What is happening to our environment?

As Myanmar’s largest city continues to grow, Yangon is experiencing serious environmental problems that threaten both people and nature. Understanding these challenges is an important step toward protecting Yangon for future generations.

Water Pollution

The pollution of water is one of Yangon's major environmental problems. A number of lakes, rivers and channels in the city are polluted by untreated waste water, sewage water, solid waste etc. Water is drained into nearby rivers from industrial factories, and urban centers without treatment in a number of cases; these streams contain harmful pollutants, hence they pose a threat to aquatic resources and affect water quality. Additionally, some residents of the city dispose plastic and other solid waste directly into drain channels and waterways, therefore increasing pollution and clogging of drainage systems.


The pollution of water has implications on the ecosystem and public health in Yangon. Water pollution can lead to various health hazards to the citizens by diseases such as skin infection, cholera, diarrhea etc especially if it is spread via poor sanitation system. Pollution of water leads to killing fish populations in lakes and rivers and is hazardous for ecosystem. Environment experts have recommended that treatment of waste water and waste disposal should be implemented to tackle the problem of water pollution in Yangon.

Climate Change

Climate change is now a considerable environmental issue in Yangon. Due to the increasing global temperature trend, climate change affects Myanmar with increase of heat level, severity of storm events, and unpredictability of rainfall regimes. Researchers found that Yangon average temperature increased 0.16C per decade since 1970, which clearly demonstrates an upward trend in warming. Experts have found that patterns of rainfall will become more extreme and occur more frequently; greater quantities will fall more intensely in a given time. The mean annual rainfall in Yangon is 2700 mm, which is already high, and the increase in extreme rainfall and heat waves as the consequence of climate change is inevitable. Since Yangon is located in a low-lying coastal area, it is also subjected to the risk of sea-level rise and storm surge that directly relate to climate change; they are seriously posing public health and transport services, housing and infrastructure to be threatened.

Flooding

Flooding is a major environmental problem in Yangon. The city is highly susceptible to flooding because it is located in a coastal lowland area, neighboring several rivers and streams. Heavy precipitation in Yangon's monsoon season commonly exceeds the capacity of the city's existing drainage systems, resulting in inundated streets, homes and communities. A major cause of flood aggravation in Yangon's localities include blocked drains, lack of proper city planning and pollution of waterways with solid waste. One researcher notes that 50 mm and above rainfall recorded in the locality can trigger devastating floods, indicating how easily Yangon is submerged during heavy rains.


Flooding impacts transport, commerce, education and public life all over Yangon. The roads become unpassable while homes and civic infrastructure get damaged from the rising water. Floods contribute to health hazards as water contaminated with sewage can cause diseases like diarrhea and dengue fever. According to some research, both climate change and rapid urbanization are responsible for the increased magnitude and incidence of flooding in Yangon, and therefore the effective flood management system with adequate drainage infrastructure for the city has become an issue of critical importance.

Air Pollution

In Yangon, air pollution has become a grave issue as a consequence of heavy traffic, factory emissions, construction works and incineration. The massive amount of vehicles in Yangon city has created an extensive quantity of smoke, a number of poisonous gases and fine particulate, which cause the air to deteriorate in quality. The diesel generators and factories, mainly in urban areas and populated localities, have emitted polluting factors in the air and smoke fromburning of garbage and dry leaves in dry seasons have worsened the air quality of Yangon further.


The dirty air quality has had negative impacts on the people, specifically in terms of health. Such conditions have primarily affected children, the elderly and people with certain diseases (respiratory illness), leading to the risk of asthma, lung disorders, coughs and other lung disorders. According to environmental specialists, it may even escalate in the future due to accelerating urbanization and rising trend of vehicle usage and will continue until concrete measures against environmental pollution and use of non-polluting systems of transportation are adopted and promoted.
