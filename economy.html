Yangon’s Economic and Social Crisis

Yangon is facing rising prices, unemployment, power outages, and growing poverty. Many families now struggle to afford basic necessities as wages fail to keep up with the increasing cost of living.

Explore how the crisis has affected daily life, jobs, education, and public safety across Yangon.

Introduction

The economic and social crisis has significantly worsened since the 2021 military coup in Myanmar, and has had particularly devastating consequences in Yangon. The cost of goods such as food, rent, electricity and education has surged, while at the same time the average wage has fallen too far below minimum subsistence levels. Many households who were once secure and prosperous have had their economic stability erased, while close to half of the population of Yangon are thought to now be living beneath the poverty line.

Rising Cost of Living in Yangon

The increasing cost of living is making life more difficult for most people in Yangon, with poor public services, intermittent electricity shortages and limited jobs impacting on daily routines. While some families have been forced to pull their children out of school, cut back on meals and turn to loans just to scrape by, day to day life has now become solely centered around the effort of coping with this difficult situation.


The cost of food has been particularly impacted, with staple foods such as rice, oil and eggs now more expensive to purchase, while fuel and other essentials have similarly soared in price, in part driving up the cost of transport, while the unreliable public transport system also makes commutes longer and more costly. The costs of renting an apartment and paying bills continue to rise steeply in Yangon, though household incomes remain unchanged.

Financial Pressure

Households in Yangon are facing increased financial strain as inflation far outpaces earning. It is becoming harder to manage day-to-day costs such as food, rent, schooling, transport and health services. Many people borrow money or use up their savings to cover costs, which can lead to long-term debt and financial instability. Many middle-income households were previously able to live in comfort, but they have been forced to draw down from their savings, and are now living by either taking out loans through informal markets, or relying on the relatives living abroad.

Crime and Declining Public Trust

A decrease in public faith in institutions means that few people turn to authorities for help with crime, theft being increasingly widespread as public services are stretched. Because they lack faith that authorities will act appropriately, many crimes and acts of theft go unreported to the police, contributing to public insecurity, fear and erosion of trust.


Fear has increased for people traveling late at night or simply walking through their neighborhoods, while an increase in crime and petty theft has accompanied the deterioration of economic circumstances. Public trust has simultaneously continued to decline over whether public services such as the education system and welfare provisions are able to respond to the needs of ordinary people, exacerbating isolation and uncertainty.

Impact on Education

Access to education has also been heavily impacted by the growing economic crisis, with many parents unable to afford the rising costs of transport to school, the fees themselves, and simply the day to day expense of keeping their child in education. Some children have been pulled from school in order to help their families earn money through working.

The political situation has also impacted education, making many institutions reluctant to open their doors to students due to safety fears, and reduced public faith. Universities and other educational bodies have remained under a cloud of uncertainty regarding their capacity and curriculum in the future.

Employment and Low Wages

Job opportunities have shrunk across Yangon, and many are earning salaries insufficient to cover their essential living costs, while working long and unproductive hours simply to try and get by. This has led some factory workers to strike for better pay, in a bid to keep their heads above water.


Many companies, from small retail businesses to larger manufacturing operations, have either been forced to cut their workforce significantly or cease operating entirely due to unstable economic and political conditions, devalued currency, and a sharp reduction in spending power. Tourism and the hotel industry, as well as retail businesses, have also suffered tremendously.

Urban Poverty and Declining Public Services

Yangon has become afflicted by deep levels of urban poverty, with public services failing to meet demand, impacting virtually all areas of life. Survival and making ends meet have taken precedence over education and employment, or indeed security, as city life under economic collapse continues to present its challenges. Roads and transport, sanitation, and electricity all deteriorate further across different parts of the city, with lower income areas suffering the worst from these issues. Charities, community groups and volunteers are working tirelessly to deliver food parcels to poor families, education support and other emergency help.
